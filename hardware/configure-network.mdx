---
title: Configure the robot network
description: Give a wired robot its address, with a static IP or over DHCP, and see it come Online in the platform.
---

Cabling a robot is not enough for the platform to see it. It needs an address on your network, and you set that address on the robot itself, with a screen and a keyboard plugged into the back. Do it before you screw the robot into its rack: those sockets are much easier to reach on a table.

<Note>
  This procedure applies to the latest generation of Kapptivate robots, on the models with display output. The mini cellular robot has none: reach it over a serial line instead, see [log in on a robot without display output](/hardware/headless-login). For an older robot, contact support@kapptivate.com.
</Note>

Every robot goes through this page, web, cellular, and smartphone alike. Smartphone robots then need each phone prepared, on [Android](/hardware/prepare-android) or on [iPhone](/hardware/prepare-iphone).

## Which flows to open

If your robot goes straight out to the internet, you have no flows to open. It finds Kapptivate on its first boot.

On a filtered network, your IT department opens the flows from the robot toward Kapptivate. The IP requirements document lists them, and support@kapptivate.com sends it to you.

Those flows start from the robot's own address, so the order matters here. Give the robot its IP first, then have the flows opened from that address. Until they are open, the robot shows **Offline**, exactly as it would with a wrong address.

<Note>
  The robot opens every one of these connections itself. Nothing has to reach it from the internet.
</Note>

## Static IP or DHCP

A static IP is the recommended setup, and the procedure below sets one. The address never moves, your flows open from it, and you still have it the day the robot goes quiet.

DHCP works too. On a simple LAN, leave the robot on DHCP: it takes a lease at boot and reaches the platform on its own. Only check that the RJ45 cable sits in Ethernet port 1, 2, or 3, because port 4 does not work. Nothing else to plug in, so skip the steps below and go straight to [check that the robot is online](#check-that-the-robot-is-online).

## Before you start

- The robot is unpacked, powered, and cabled to the network, but not yet screwed into the rack: see [install a robot](/hardware/install-robot).
- A USB keyboard, a USB mouse, a screen, and an HDMI cable. None of these ship with the robot.
- The robot's credentials. Ask support@kapptivate.com if you don't have them.
- The static IP, mask, gateway, and DNS server addresses, from your IT department.
- Access to the Kapptivate platform, where you read the robot's status at the end.

## Set the static IP

<Steps>
  <Step title="Check which port the cable is in">
    The RJ45 cable belongs in the Ethernet port labeled **1**. Ports 2 and 3 work too, but you then configure `lan2` or `lan3` further down instead of `lan1`. Port 4 does not work.

    <Frame caption="How the Ethernet ports are numbered">
      ![The numbered Ethernet ports at the back of the robot](/images/hardware/configure-network-11.webp)
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect the peripherals">
    Plug the keyboard, mouse, and HDMI cable into the back of the robot.

    <Frame caption="Peripheral connections at the back">
      ![Keyboard, mouse, and HDMI cable plugged into the back of the robot](/images/hardware/configure-network-01.webp)
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Power on and log in">
    Turn the robot on with the front button. At the login screen, enter the robot's credentials, the ones support@kapptivate.com gave you. You land on a Linux desktop.

    <Frame caption="The front power button">
      ![The power button on the front panel of the robot](/images/hardware/configure-network-02.webp)
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open the connection editor">
    Click the network configuration icon and select **Edit Connections**.

    <Frame caption="Edit Connections in the network menu">
      ![The network menu open on the Edit Connections entry](/images/hardware/configure-network-03.webp)
    </Frame>

    The connection here is named `lan1`, and yours may carry another name, **Wired connection 1** for example. The number follows the port, so pick the connection matching the port you checked in step 1, then edit it.

    <Frame caption="Select the connection and edit it">
      ![The connection list with lan1 selected](/images/hardware/configure-network-04.webp)
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the General tab">
    In **General**, **Connect automatically with priority** has to be checked, with the value `-999`. Check it if it isn't.

    <Frame caption="General tab">
      ![The General tab of the connection editor](/images/hardware/configure-network-05.webp)
    </Frame>

    <Frame caption="Connect automatically with priority -999">
      ![Connect automatically with priority set to -999](/images/hardware/configure-network-06.webp)
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Switch to manual addressing">
    Open **IPV4 Settings** and change the method from **Automatic (DHCP)** to **Manual**.

    <Frame caption="IPV4 Settings tab">
      ![The IPV4 Settings tab](/images/hardware/configure-network-07.webp)
    </Frame>

    <Frame caption="Switch from DHCP to Manual">
      ![The method dropdown set to Manual](/images/hardware/configure-network-08.webp)
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter the addresses">
    Click **Add**, then enter the IP address, mask, and gateway from your IT department. Fill the **DNS servers** field too. Without DNS servers the robot resolves no name and reaches Kapptivate by IP only.

    <Frame caption="Enter the address, mask, gateway, and DNS">
      ![The address, mask, gateway, and DNS fields filled in](/images/hardware/configure-network-09.webp)
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Click **Save**. The connection comes back up with its new address.

    <Frame caption="Save the connection">
      ![The Save button of the connection editor](/images/hardware/configure-network-10.webp)
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Reboot the robot">
    Reboot the robot from the Linux desktop. Every change you made takes effect on the way back up, and the robot returns on its final address.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Check that the robot is online

Open the Kapptivate platform: **Agents** is where the platform lists your robots. Find yours. It shows **Online** once it reaches the platform, and that is your signal to rack it.

If it stays **Offline**, work through [network troubleshooting](/hardware/network-troubleshooting): the checks there start with the cable and the port, then the flows, then DNS resolution.

A smartphone robot is not finished at this point. The robot is online, but no phone accepts a test until you prepare it.

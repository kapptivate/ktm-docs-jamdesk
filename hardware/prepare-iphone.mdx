---
title: Prepare an iPhone
description: Set up an iPhone for a smartphone robot, from the iOS settings to the pairing session with Kapptivate support.
---

Preparing an iPhone splits in two. The settings are yours: a handful of toggles that stop iOS from locking, updating, or refusing the cable. The activation is not: Apple requires the device to be registered before it accepts automated testing, and that part goes through Kapptivate support.

Plan for it. The Apple registration takes 24 to 72 hours, so a new iPhone is not testable the same day.

## Before you start

- The robot is installed, with its USB hub powered on: see [install a robot](/hardware/install-robot).
- The robot's agent shows **Online** in the platform: see [configure the robot network](/hardware/configure-network).
- The iPhone runs a [supported iOS version](/questions/devices-os-versions).
- A Lightning or USB-C cable for the phone. The hub ships with the robot, the phone cables do not.

<Warning>
  The iPhone needs internet access, over Wi-Fi or the mobile network, at all times. iOS checks the certificates the platform uses online, so without a connection the device never shows up correctly in Kapptivate.
</Warning>

## First boot

Turn the phone on and go through the iOS setup: pick **English** and your country, skip every account, and connect it to Wi-Fi. Every label quoted below is the English one.

<Warning>
  Do not set a passcode, Face ID, or Touch ID. The screen has to stay unlocked for the platform to use the phone.
</Warning>

## Set up iOS

<Steps>
  <Step title="Turn off automatic updates">
    In **Settings > General > Software Update > Automatic Updates**, turn off every **iOS Updates** entry. The platform supports [iOS 11 to 26](/questions/devices-os-versions), and an update can push the phone past the versions it covers.

    <Frame caption="Automatic Updates, off">
      ![The iOS Updates toggle turned off](/images/hardware/prepare-iphone-01.webp)
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Stop the screen from locking">
    In **Settings > Display & Brightness > Auto-Lock**, select **Never**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enable the Web Inspector">
    In **Settings > Safari > Advanced**, turn **Web Inspector** on.

    <Frame caption="Web Inspector, on">
      ![The Web Inspector toggle turned on](/images/hardware/prepare-iphone-02.webp)
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Allow wired accessories (iOS 26 and later)">
    In **Settings > Privacy & Security > Wired accessories**, select **Always allow**. Earlier versions of iOS don't have this setting, so skip the step there.

    <Frame caption="Privacy & Security, then Wired accessories">
      ![The Privacy and Security screen](/images/hardware/prepare-iphone-03.webp)
      ![Wired accessories set to Always allow](/images/hardware/prepare-iphone-04.webp)
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect the phone to the robot">
    Plug the phone into a free USB port of the hub. If it was already plugged in, unplug it and plug it back: the popup only fires on connection.

    When the iPhone asks whether to trust the computer, tap **Trust**. Dismiss any other popup with whichever option closes it.

    <Frame caption="The trust popup on the iPhone">
      ![The popup asking whether to trust the computer, with the Trust option](/images/hardware/prepare-iphone-05.webp)
    </Frame>
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Finish with support

These three stages are Apple's requirements and they run in this order. All three go through support.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Support reads the device identifier">
    Contact support to say the iPhone is connected and ready. If you went through the steps above and tapped **Trust**, there is nothing left to do on your side: support reads the device identifier (UDID) directly from the robot.

    Tell support if the trust popup never appeared. They can make it appear again during the session below.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Apple registers the device">
    Support registers the UDID with Apple, which is what allows automated testing on that specific iPhone. Count 24 to 72 hours.

    This stage only concerns an iPhone that has never been used with Kapptivate. A device already registered goes straight to the session.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pairing and Developer Mode, with support">
    Ask support for a 5-minute slot to finish the setup. During the session you confirm the popups on the phone, then check one last setting:

    1. If the trust popup never appeared, support triggers it. Tap **Trust**.
    2. Support then triggers the Developer Mode activation popup. Confirm it. The iPhone may reboot afterwards.
    3. Check that **Enable UI Automation** is on in the Developer Mode settings.

    If iOS asks for a restart at any point, restart the phone and leave it connected to the robot.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Check that it worked

Open the Kapptivate platform, find the robot's agent under **Agents**, and open it. The iPhone appears in its device list and shows **Available** once it is ready to take tests.

A device only becomes available when its own status is **Available** and its agent is **Online**. If the phone shows nothing, check the agent first.

If the phone is registered, paired, and still absent, the two usual causes are a lost internet connection and a dismissed trust popup. Check the Wi-Fi, then unplug and replug the cable to bring the popup back.

A SIM that stops running tests points to one of three suspects: the card, the slot that holds it, or the network behind it. Replacing the card only fixes the first. This page tells them apart, starting from what the platform shows. It covers the SIMs of cellular robots.

## Before you go on site

Open the robot in **Agents**, then its **Connected devices** tab. For each slot, note the SIM and its number, its **Status**, and its **Network** value.

<Frame caption="The Connected devices tab of a 4G robot">
  ![The Connected devices tab of the robot kapp-demo-4g-1, which is Online. The Slots table shows SIM 4274 in slot 1, Available, on LTE, with Healthy usage, 10 accesses, and 3 monitors. Slots 2 to 4 read No SIM inserted.](/images/hardware/sim-troubleshooting-slots.webp)
</Frame>

Then compare with the other SIMs of the same robot and of the same site.

- **One SIM fails on its own**: look at the card and its slot.
- **Every SIM of one operator fails on the same site**, even if they didn't go down at the same time: that operator's network on this site is the likely cause, so the investigation belongs on the network side. Nationwide outages are rare. Most of the time the outage stays local to one site.
- **Only the SIMs testing one technology fail**: that technology is likely down on the site. Say a site has one SIM running only 4G tests and one running only 3G tests. If the 4G SIM works and the 3G SIM won't register, 3G is probably down there.

## Where to start

Whatever the case, reboot the slot first. Use **Reboot slot** at the top of the SIM's page, or in the three-dot menu of its row in the **Connected devices** tab.

<Frame caption="The menu of an occupied slot, in the Connected devices tab">
  ![The row of slot 1 in the Slots table, with SIM 4274 Available on LTE. Its three-dot menu is open on Edit, Activate test-only mode, and Reboot slot.](/images/hardware/sim-troubleshooting-reboot-slot.webp)
</Frame>

| What you see | What to check | Where to go |
|---|---|---|
| **Network** stays on **Denied** | the card's IMSI | [The card is read, the network refuses it](#the-card-is-read-the-network-refuses-it) |
| The slot reads **No SIM inserted** with a card in it | the card and the slot contact | [The card is no longer detected](#the-card-is-no-longer-detected) |
| **Network** reads **Not registered** or **Searching** for more than ten minutes | activation, radio, access technology | [A SIM that won't register](#a-sim-that-wont-register) |
| The SIM reads **Unnamed**, with the status **Configuration** | its number in the platform | [Swap a SIM](/hardware/swap-sim#set-up-a-new-number) |
| The SIM looks fine, but a test fails with a message | the message itself | [SIM test error messages](/hardware/sim-test-errors) |

## The card is read, the network refuses it

The robot reads the card and the signal is good, yet the network won't register it. Replacing the card fixes this most of the time: follow [Swap a SIM](/hardware/swap-sim).

The likely cause is the operator blocking the card's IMSI. A new card carries a new IMSI, which is why the swap works. To check before you replace anything, read the IMSI in the SIM's **Settings** tab, under **SIM Details**, and make sure it isn't blocked.

## The card is no longer detected

The slot reads **No SIM inserted**, and yet the card is in. A reboot alone won't bring it back: either the card has reached the end of its life, or the slot contact is worn.

Take the card out, put it back in, then reboot the slot. If it stays invisible, [tell the card from the slot](#tell-the-card-from-the-slot).

## A SIM that won't register

Ten minutes on **Not registered** or **Searching** is a fault, not a wait. Check three things, in this order.

- **The line is active.** A new card nobody activated in the HLR won't register, and neither will a suspended line.
- **The radio reaches the robot.** If the SIM fails in two slots, compare the signal with a robot of the same model on another site. No such robot? Use a phone, held where the robot stands. If the signal is much weaker here, check that the antennas are screwed on. Move the robot if no network gets through even then.
- **The test doesn't force a missing technology.** Forcing 3G or 2G on a cell that no longer serves it usually leaves the SIM on **Not registered**, sometimes on **Searching**. Set **Access technology** to **Default** on every step of every test this SIM runs, unless a test checks 3G or 2G on purpose.

## What you need on site

Everything above runs remotely. [Telling the card from the slot](#tell-the-card-from-the-slot) is the only check that takes a site visit.

- An unlocked phone, to test the card outside the robot.
- A working SIM from the same robot, to swap the SIMs.
- For a 4G robot, a SIM ejector tool.

## Tell the card from the slot

**Swap the SIMs.** Move the failing card into the slot of a working SIM from the same robot, and that SIM into the failing slot. Reboot both slots with **Reboot slot** in the platform. If the fault follows the card, the card or its subscription is to blame: replace the card, following [Swap a SIM](/hardware/swap-sim). If it stays with the slot, the slot is.

**Test the card in a phone.** If it doesn't register, receive SMS, or complete the USSD code there either, the robot is not involved. Replace the card, following [Swap a SIM](/hardware/swap-sim).

A fault that stays with the slot goes to support@kapptivate.com, with the robot's name as the platform shows it and the slot number. Support checks the robot. An IMEI banned by the operator can also cause it.

## Who to contact

A fault that follows the card or comes from the network goes to your own teams: the HLR for a refused IMSI or an inactive line, your network team for an outage on a site. Everything else goes to support@kapptivate.com, with the robot's name as the platform shows it, the slot number, the exact message, and the time of the failure.
